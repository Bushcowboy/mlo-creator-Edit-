## 2026-10-26 (14) — Fix: "Block length does not match with its complement" when adding a game interior

- **Adding an interior on the game interiors page failed** with *Block length does not match with its complement* on a real GTA V install, and so did its export and *From a game interior* in Place props. The program read the interior's type file (and, in the export, its collision files) out of the game's archives already unpacked, then handed it to CodeWalker as if it were a packed file, which unpacked it a second time. The list itself was fine (it reads the files the right way). Now those files are handed over whole, the same way *pull a building from the game* already did (`Vanilla.Whole`).
- Tested: a new host test packs the test interiors and their collision into a real `.rpf` archive with CodeWalker and reads them back the way the program reads the game's archives: the old way fails with this same error (Mono words it *Corrupted data*), the new way adds the interior (rooms, portals, entity sets), lists its props and exports two joined interiors with their collision (32 checks). The earlier tests do not catch it because they handed the program whole files from disk. All host suites again (143). **Not tested on a real GTA V install**: tell me if Add or Export still fails, with the message.

- **New project now asks what you want to turn into an MLO**: *A building in the game* (new), *A game interior* (the casino, an apartment...), *Building files you already have*, or *A new building from scratch*.

- **Everything it needs is pulled from the game** (through CodeWalker's library): the model, its texture dictionary (from its archetype) and that one's parents from the game's `gtxd` files, the map file that places it (the placement you picked), its detail models with their textures, the block's collision that reaches it and the occlusion files with boxes near it. Files already there are left alone. The Import tab has the same as *From the game*: find by name, *Pull this one*, *Pull it again*.
- **The guided build** (top of the Import and Build tabs) lists every step in order. *Do it for me* does one; *Do every step it can* does them all: where it stands, the building out of the block, the grid, the game's collision, **a first room filling the building** on its walls with the ceiling at their top, **doors and windows found by their textures** (door, entrance, shutter; window, glass, pane) and cut out, the collision cleared out of the rooms, a ceiling light every 4.5 m, the map's plants, the walk check. Where it needs you (a room it could not find, your developer name, Export) it says *Your turn* with what to do and the tool, and **goes on by itself once you have done it**.

## 2026-10-26 (11) — The model's own textures, and props from what you imported

- **Paint with the model's textures.** The textures of your imported files (the ones on the model, and up to 48 more from its `.ytd` files) are in the Paint tab (*From the model*) and in the bench's *From the model* category, the most used first, with where they are on the model (walls, floors and roofs, ceilings). Each comes at the size it has on the model: the host measures one repeat in metres from the model's own texture coordinates, and a picture that is not square keeps its proportions on your walls. Change it with *repeats every* (kept in the project, undoable). The bench also shows the program's textures closest in colour to it.
- Their **own normal and specular maps** (the model shader's `BumpSampler` / `SpecSampler`) and its bumpiness and shine go with them; a texture without them gets a normal map made from the picture, like the built-in ones.
- **The export copies the picture itself**, not the 256 px preview: the original from your `.ytd` (or from inside the model), with its own maps, into `<prefix>_tex.ytd`, at most 1024 px (the bigger mip levels are left out, so nothing is compressed again). If the file is gone, the preview is used and the log says so.
- **Place props** tool (key 0, *Finish the inside*): pick a model, click the floor; click another prop to stand it on top (a vase on a table). With no model chosen (Esc) or with Alt: click selects, drag moves, R turns 90° (Shift+R 15°), Shift+D copies, X removes; position, height and facing in the panel. The view draws each prop's own mesh. Per building.
- **What it offers comes from the project**: *From your files* (the props your imported `.ymap` files place, most used first, and model files of yours that nothing places), *Around the building in the game* (what the game's map places within 25 m, by count), *Same game archive* (the other models of the building's `.rpf`), *From a game interior* (the game's interiors within 600 m of the building, or one found by name: its props with counts and entity sets, its wall and floor pieces left out), and *Search every game model*.
- **Export**: each prop is an entity of the interior room. For a game model, the type file that defines it goes into the interior's manifest (`itypDepArray`) so the game loads it with the interior (this uses the list of the game's models made with the game's interior list; without it the export warns). A model file of yours is copied into `stream/` with the `.ytd` that holds its textures and is defined in the interior's type file. The README lists the props.
- Tested: host (29 checks: texture info and repeat size measured on an exported model, 0.9 m brick and 1.6 m marble found again; normal maps and bumpiness found; mip-level shrinking keeps the exact pixels; the export writes the model's picture at the size asked for and its own normal map instead of the editor's stand-in; a missing texture falls back with a warning; props as entities of room 1 with the inverted turn the game uses, the type file in the manifest, your own model shipped with its texture file and defined, an unknown prop reported; suggestions from a project map file; meshes of your own models without the game), interiors (3 more: an interior's props with entity sets, interiors near a spot, which type file defines a model), editor in Node (45: ids, sizes, proportions, shine, nearest colours, the wall's texture coordinates, the export references; placing, stacking, picking, turning, dragging and undo, copy, remove, eraser, view meshes, export list), browser (25: the Paint tab section, the bench, *repeats every*, placing props from the files, an interior and a search, stacking, selecting, turning, the export spec), and the earlier suites again (host 94, browser 75). Also built with C# 5 rules (the Windows build). **Not tested in game**, nor with a real GTA V install: the game's archive, the props around the building, the interior list and the search need it.

## 2026-10-26 (10) — Vaulted ceilings, beams and columns

- **Room ceilings** (select a room with Select, *Ceiling*): *Flat*, *Vaulted* (two slopes up to a ridge), *Barrel* (a round vault), *Tray* (a raised middle with a step all round, set the border), *Shed* (one slope, with a wall on the high side). Set the rise, which way the ridge runs (the long side by default, or along x / y) and the texture. The gable ends close the space above the walls. The sloped ceiling gets collision triangles; the room's box in the MLO grows to hold it.
- **Ceiling beams** per room: *Across*, *Along* or *Both* (a coffered grid), how many, width, depth, texture. Under a vault they follow it: across gives ribs or rafters, along gives purlins and a ridge beam.
- **Column tool** (key 8, *Finish the inside > Columns and beams*): click the floor to stand a column; square or round (16 sides), width, texture, base and capital, height (0 = to the ceiling, also under a vault: it reaches the slope above it). Collision box. Click a column again to remove it.
- **Beam tool** (key 9): click where a beam starts and where it ends; straight or 45° (Shift: any angle); width, depth, texture, and its top at the ceiling or at a height you give. A beam low enough to walk into (bottom under 2.3 m) gets a turned collision box. Alt+click removes one; the Eraser takes columns and beams too. Columns and beams are listed with all their settings in the tool panels.
- Per building, like the rooms. For imported-model projects (the tools); the shapes are built for grabbed-house projects too. The editor's view hides ceilings as before (tick *Ceiling* in the view options to see them).
- Make sure the building's roof is higher than the vault, or the ceiling shows through it outside.
- Tested: builder in Node (20 checks: ridge height and direction, no flat ceiling left, gable ends closed and facing in, barrel end with 16 segments, shed wall, tray step, beam positions, ribs, column reaching the slope, beam collision, no zero-area collision triangles); the real exporter + CodeWalker (model and collision reach the ridge, room box holds it); browser checks of the tools and panels. **Not tested in game.**

## 2026-10-26 (6) — Timecycle editor

- Paint tab, *Timecycle*: *Usual* (what MLO Creator always used), *The game's* (any of the game's timecycle modifiers by name; *Read the game's list* reads every `timecycle_mods` file of your install, plain or binary XML), or *My own*.
- *My own*: sliders for exposure, daylight through windows, interior ambient, ambient from below and above (with colours), bloom and its threshold, ambient occlusion, reflection ambient, and light on people (with colour). Each value has a tick: unticked values are not written, so the weather's timecycle decides them. *Start from* copies all the values of one of the game's modifiers (values without a slider come along unchanged).
- On export your own modifier is written to `timecycle_mods_<resource>.xml` at the resource root as `<prefix>_tc`, listed in `files` and registered with `data_file 'TIMECYCLEMOD_FILE'`; the interior's room uses it.
- Variable names and the file format come from the game's own modifier files (checked against the game's `v_laund_rm`); the `TIMECYCLEMOD_FILE` data file type is in FiveM's documentation.
- Tested: reading a game modifier file; the exported file, `numMods`, fxmanifest lines and the room's timecycle hash (read back with CodeWalker); host routes; browser checks. **Not tested in game**: how the values look.

## 2026-10-26 (4) — Wall colour schemes as entity sets

- Paint tab, *Wall colours (entity sets)*: add colour schemes; in each, swap any texture used inside (pick a texture in the bench, then *Use picked*). *Show* previews a scheme in the 3D view.
- On export, every surface whose texture a scheme swaps leaves the interior's always-there model and goes into one model per scheme (`<prefix>_pnt0.ydr` = the colours as painted, `_pnt1`, `_pnt2`...). Each is an entity set of the MLO named `<prefix>_paint_<scheme>`, e.g. `bushcowboy_casino_paint_navy`. The default set (`<prefix>_paint_default`, renameable) is on by default in the placement map; switching is left to your script. The README lists the sets with an `ActivateInteriorEntitySet` example.
- Surfaces no scheme touches stay in the interior model. Textures repeat at their own size in each scheme. A scheme that swaps nothing is not exported.
- Smoother normal maps for wallpaper and brushed metal.
- Tested: an editor-built export with two schemes, read back with CodeWalker: 3 entity sets with one part each in room 1, the always-there part as the only MLO entity, the swapped textures in the scheme models, only the default set on in the ymap, README section; browser checks of the panel. **Not tested in game**: switching the sets live.

## 2026-10-26 (3) — Normal and specular maps, 24 new materials

- Every texture is exported with a normal map (`<name>_n`) and a specular map (`<name>_s`) and uses the game's `normal_spec` shader, with per-vertex tangents worked out from the UVs. Mortar, grout, plank gaps and panel joints are sunk in; tiles, marble and metal shine, plaster and carpet stay matt. Each kind of surface has its own bumpiness and shine.
- New materials: marble (white, black, green, cream), terrazzo (grey, warm), polished concrete, herringbone parquet (oak, walnut), beadboard (white, green), wallpaper (stripes, diamond prints), patterned casino and hotel carpets, brushed steel, brass, gold, black metal, and black, gold and walnut trims.
- Paint tab, *Materials in the game*: normal maps on or off (off = smaller files), and *Flip the green channel* in case bumps look raised instead of sunk in game (the one part of the normal-map convention that could not be checked here). The texture bench shows each material's normal and specular map.
- Photos and your own images get maps made from their brightness.
- Tested: every material and its maps generate without errors; an export made by the editor's own code, read back with CodeWalker, has `normal_spec` with the normal and specular textures, the right texture usages, and tangents in the vertices. **Not tested in game**: how strong the bumps and shine look, and the green channel direction.
